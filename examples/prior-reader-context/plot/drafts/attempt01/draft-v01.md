The survey brig *Wayfinder* drove east under a steady northwest wind on her starboard quarter. A long following swell lifted the stern, passed beneath Eda's boots, and sent the bow down into clear morning light.

At sixteen, Eda Venn was the youngest navigation cadet aboard and the only one currently trusted with the dawn course. She kept the compass card steady while the watch officer worked the rail behind her.

A red point opened astern, bright enough to mark the horizon. It hung for one breath and vanished.

Eda waited for the watch officer to speak. He did not. She found the compass again, recovered the count she had lost, and kept the flare to herself.

By afternoon the survey brig *Wayfinder* was still driving east under the steady northwest wind, the breeze firm on her starboard quarter. The same long following swell lifted the stern and lowered the bow in an unbroken rhythm.

Hale set Eda's calculation folio beside her elbow. Three columns ended halfway down the page.

"What interrupted you?" he asked.

Eda kept her eyes on the last complete figure. "I saw a light astern."

"A ship?"

"I couldn't tell."

Hale closed the folio. "Then I'll check the afternoon signal log."

She let him leave without saying that the light had been red, or that she knew the shape of a distress flare.
